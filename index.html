Case Study

Scaling Apogee to a $500m Exit

Context

Paul joined Apogee when the business still relied heavily on manual workflows, spreadsheet-driven processes and basic legacy service tooling, but had strong ambition for national expansion and acquisition-led growth.

The challenge was not simply to improve IT, but to build the operational structure, controls and platforms that would allow the organisation to scale without losing visibility, pace or discipline.

Starting point ~20 staff | ~£5-6m revenue | one Croydon office
Strategic challenge Build scalable platforms and operating discipline for national growth

Action

  • Built Silo ERP to manage the full sales-to-delivery lifecycle including quoting, solution progression, provisioning and billing readiness
  • Evolved CBS service management workflows, reporting and controls to strengthen governance and operational insight
  • Integrated Radius CRM with core operational platforms to reduce duplication and improve visibility across pipeline, delivery and service
  • Introduced reconciliation, purchasing control, lease document and EDI capabilities to improve financial discipline and operational consistency
  • Worked alongside the CEO and SLT to shape the operating model supporting national scale and acquisition integration
  • Led technology due diligence, post-acquisition integration and hands-on stabilisation of acquired businesses
  • Supported culture alignment and transition within newly acquired firms, often in an embedded leadership capacity
  • Helped drive the transition from analogue document solutions towards digital software-led services

Impact

The result was an integrated operating environment that gave the business stronger control, cleaner data, better reporting and the ability to scale nationally with confidence.

  • Revenue growth from £6m to approximately £170m
  • 600+ staff across 14 UK offices plus European locations
  • 14 acquisitions integrated into a unified operational platform and operating model
  • Improved visibility across sales, delivery, service and field engineering activity
  • Apogee acquired by Hewlett-Packard for approximately $500m
  • Core systems developed in this period remain central to the business today
Case Study

Building Tinder Corporation

Context

Tinder Corporation was founded to bring enterprise-grade IT strategy, technology capability and operating model discipline to the SME market. From day one, the aim was to create a business with stronger structure, better systems and clearer governance than most businesses of its size.

As the company evolved, it expanded beyond managed IT and telecoms into higher-stakes telecom infrastructure delivery, requiring a significant step-up in project control, compliance and reporting maturity.

Founder phase Built from zero with ERP, SOPs, contracts and operating model in place
Growth phase Expanded into telecom infrastructure and complex client delivery environments

Action

  • Built Centric ERP before launch to manage operations from the outset rather than bolting systems on later
  • Designed the full operating model including workflows, service governance, legal/commercial structure and reporting controls
  • Expanded into telecom infrastructure delivery for Hyperoptic, Virgin Media and BT OpenReach via Morrison Utilities
  • Implemented PMO capability, contractor controls, SHEQ/compliance frameworks and client-platform workflows including Salesforce and iAuditor
  • Led development of the Quality Management System and achieved ISO 9001 with a genuinely embedded governance approach
  • Introduced automation and LLM-driven workflows directly into Centric for document generation, knowledge access and process acceleration
  • Built data-processing pipelines to bridge gaps where client systems had no usable API access
  • Delivered SLT skills development to improve leadership maturity and organisational capability

Impact

The business developed into a structured, platform-led operation capable of delivering within demanding infrastructure environments while maintaining governance and operational control.

  • Scaled to a 45-person workforce across office, field and contractor capability
  • Delivered for Hyperoptic, Virgin Media, Morrison Utilities and BT OpenReach environments
  • Built stronger PMO, compliance and evidence-based operational discipline
  • Created automation and AI-enabled workflows that reduced manual effort and improved consistency
  • Established a more mature operating environment than is typical for a business of that size
Operating Philosophy

How transformation is approached

Clarity before complexity

Establish a clear operating model, decision framework and accountability structure before introducing tools or technology.

Technology as an enabler, not the strategy

Systems should remove friction from operations, improve flow and reduce manual effort rather than add complexity.

Operational visibility drives performance

Leaders should be able to see what is happening in the business in near real time through reliable data and structured reporting.

People first, systems second

Technology only works when teams understand the purpose, priorities and outcomes expected of them.

Decisive leadership when needed

Organisations scale when leaders are willing to make difficult decisions quickly, calmly and clearly.

Applied AI with practical value

Use AI where it creates tangible operational advantage - knowledge access, document automation, workflow acceleration and decision support.
