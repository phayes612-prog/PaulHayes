Case Study

Scaling Apogee to a $500m Exit

A platform and operating-model story, not just an IT story. The challenge was to create enough structure, visibility and control for acquisition-led national growth without slowing the business down.

Apogee - enterprise platform and integration backbone

Technology, operations and integration leadership supporting long-term scale, control and eventual exit value.

ERP CRM Service platform Acquisition integration Operational governance
~28x Revenue growth supported from approximately £6m to approximately £170m
14 Acquisitions integrated into a more unified operating environment
600+ Staff supported as the business scaled nationally
~$500m Acquisition value when the business was bought by HP

Context

Paul joined Apogee when the business was still relatively small, heavily reliant on manual workflows, spreadsheet-driven processes and legacy tooling, but had serious ambition for national expansion and acquisition-led growth.

The need was broader than IT improvement. The business required an operational backbone - systems, controls, reporting and leadership structure - that could support growth without losing visibility or discipline.

What was built

  • Built Silo ERP to manage the sales-to-delivery lifecycle including quoting, progression, provisioning and billing readiness.
  • Evolved CBS service workflows, controls and reporting to improve governance and operational insight.
  • Integrated Radius CRM with operational platforms to reduce duplication and improve visibility from pipeline through service.
  • Introduced reconciliation, purchasing control, lease documentation and EDI capability to strengthen financial and operational discipline.

Leadership contribution

  • Worked closely with the CEO and senior team on the operating model needed for national scale.
  • Led technology due diligence and post-acquisition integration activity.
  • Provided embedded stabilisation and transition support within acquired businesses.
  • Helped support the shift from analogue document solutions towards more digital and software-led services.
Starting point Approximately 20 staff, one Croydon office, around £5m to £6m revenue, with limited platform maturity.
End state supported 600+ staff, multi-site operation, 14 acquisitions integrated, stronger process control and cleaner reporting foundations.
Operational effect Better visibility across sales, delivery, service and engineering activity, with less manual fragmentation and stronger control points.
Strategic significance The platform and operating discipline created during this period helped underpin a business that later exited at approximately $500m.
Why this matters: this is not framed as "implemented systems". It is evidence of building the operating backbone that allowed a business to scale from roughly £6m to roughly £170m, absorb 14 acquisitions and still function with enough clarity and control to support a major exit.
Case Study

Building Tinder Corporation

A founder-led build focused on creating a more mature, better-structured operating environment than most businesses of similar size - then extending that into telecom infrastructure delivery with tighter compliance and operational control.

Tinder Corporation - platform-led growth from day one

Built from zero with ERP, SOPs, governance and operating structure in place early rather than bolted on later.

Founder-led build ERP Telecom infrastructure PMO and compliance Automation and AI
45 People across office, field and contractor capability
5 years ISO 9001 certification and ongoing compliance successfully guided
3 major environments Hyperoptic, Virgin Media, and Morrison Utilities / BT OpenReach related delivery
Day 1 platform Centric ERP and operating model built before launch

Context

Tinder was founded to bring stronger technology, process and operating discipline into the SME market. The ambition from the beginning was to create a business with better structure, systems and governance than is typical at that scale.

As it evolved, the business expanded beyond managed IT and telecoms into more demanding telecom infrastructure delivery, increasing the need for PMO capability, compliance maturity and client-platform discipline.

What was built

  • Built Centric ERP before launch so operations had a backbone from day one.
  • Designed workflows, service governance, commercial structure and reporting controls early.
  • Implemented PMO capability, contractor controls, SHEQ and client-platform workflow discipline.
  • Developed the quality management system and led achievement of ISO 9001.

How it evolved

  • Expanded into telecom infrastructure delivery for Hyperoptic, Virgin Media and Morrison Utilities / BT OpenReach linked environments.
  • Introduced automation and LLM-driven workflows directly into Centric for knowledge access and document generation.
  • Built data-processing methods to bridge operational gaps where client systems lacked usable API access.
  • Developed leadership capability and management structure as complexity increased.
Founder phase Built from zero with ERP, SOPs, contracts and operating model deliberately established early.
Growth phase Expanded into more complex delivery settings requiring tighter control, evidence, PMO rhythm and compliance discipline.
Operating effect Created a more structured and platform-led business than is typical for a company of that size, with better governance and clearer operational visibility.
Modernisation effect Used automation and AI in practical operational ways rather than as a gimmick - document acceleration, workflow support and knowledge access.
Why this matters: this case study shows founder-level operating design, not just business ownership. The evidence is in building a working ERP-led operating environment before launch, scaling to a 45-person organisation, supporting regulated client environments, maintaining ISO 9001 for five years and introducing practical AI into live workflows.
Operating Philosophy

How transformation is approached

Clarity before complexity

Establish a clear operating model, decision framework and accountability structure before adding tools or change programmes.

Technology as an enabler

Systems should remove friction from operations, improve flow and reduce manual effort rather than create another layer of noise.

Visibility drives performance

Leadership teams should be able to see what is happening through reliable data, sensible reporting and clear operational rhythm.

People first, systems second

Technology only lands properly when people understand the purpose, priorities and outcomes expected of them.

Decisive leadership when needed

Businesses scale more effectively when leaders are willing to make difficult calls calmly, clearly and without delay.

Applied AI with practical value

Use AI where it creates operational advantage - workflow acceleration, document automation, knowledge access and decision support.

Transformation Playbook

How I approach the first 90 days

Days 1-30

Understand the system

  • Meet leadership and operational teams.
  • Map the operating model and core value streams.
  • Assess systems, reporting and data visibility.
  • Identify friction points and delivery bottlenecks.
  • Clarify strategic priorities with CEO and board.

Days 30-60

Create clarity and alignment

  • Define the target operating model.
  • Establish governance and decision frameworks.
  • Align technology architecture with operational needs.
  • Identify automation and data opportunities.
  • Stabilise higher-risk delivery areas.

Days 60-90

Drive early momentum

  • Launch priority transformation initiatives.
  • Implement reporting and performance visibility.
  • Begin platform or workflow improvements.
  • Strengthen leadership alignment and accountability.
  • Communicate progress and build momentum.
Core focus: create clarity, remove operational friction, and build systems and management rhythm that allow strong teams to scale.
